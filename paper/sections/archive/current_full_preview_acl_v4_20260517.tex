\begin{abstract}
Multi-hop question answering requires retrieval systems to surface
evidence that is not only individually relevant to a question, but also
connected across documents and covering the entities and relations needed
by a downstream reader. We present \methodname{}, a graph-based retrieval
framework that models multi-hop retrieval as query-conditioned evidence
propagation over a document-grounded OpenIE graph. \methodname{} builds a
multi-layer OpenIE graph at indexing time, runs query-local fact-level
personalized PageRank to obtain an evidence flow, and enhances the final
ranked evidence list through relation-grounded expansion and
coverage-aware reranking. Experiments on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and
MuSiQue compare \methodname{} with dense, graph-based, and
chain-oriented retrieval baselines under a unified GPT-4o-mini reader.
The results show consistent gains in retrieval quality and downstream QA,
with ablations confirming the contributions of local evidence
propagation, relation-grounded expansion, and coverage-aware reranking.
\end{abstract}
