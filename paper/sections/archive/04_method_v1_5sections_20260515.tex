\section{Method: \methodname{}}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview: Evidence Flow over a Query-Local Graph}
\label{sec:method:overview}

We treat multi-hop retrieval as the construction of a document-level evidence
chain, rather than the independent ranking of documents. Given a question $q$,
a corpus $\mathcal{D}$, and a pre-extracted fact substrate $\mathcal{F}$
derived from $\mathcal{D}$ during offline indexing, \methodname{} returns an
evidence set $E_K \subseteq \mathcal{D}$ of size $K$ for the reader. The
formation principle of $E_K$ is not that its documents are individually
highly ranked against $q$, but that the set is constructed to prioritize
connectivity through transitions that are witnessed by facts in
$\mathcal{F}$.

The method operates on a \emph{query-local document graph}
$G_q = (V_q, A_q)$, instantiated per question rather than queried from a
static pre-built knowledge graph. Vertices $V_q \subseteq \mathcal{D}$ are
documents activated by the question, and an arc
$(d_i, d_j) \in A_q$ is admitted only when facts in $\mathcal{F}$ provide
a witness for the document-to-document movement implied by $q$. This
admission rule is the central mechanism of \methodname{}: chain
connectivity is a property of the retrieval graph itself, not a relation
that a downstream module is asked to reconstruct over a flat passage pool.

Four consequences follow from this view. First, the fact substrate is
built once at indexing time and reused across questions
(\S\ref{sec:method:substrate}); at query time, no language model is invoked
to perform OpenIE extraction over $\mathcal{D}$ or to generate evidence
chains on retrieved text. Second, the activation of $G_q$ is anchored by
entities mentioned in the question, and the entry signal from dense or
lexical retrieval only seeds this activation rather than dictating the
final evidence (\S\ref{sec:method:anchor}). Third, the movement of evidence
through $G_q$ is gated by fact witnesses, which makes each chain auditable
back to source facts and their provenance in $\mathcal{D}$
(\S\ref{sec:method:transition}).
Fourth, the reader receives $E_K$ under a chain-integrity boundary that
prioritizes witnessed connectivity and admits residual documents when
they provide evidence for still-open obligations induced by $q$
(\S\ref{sec:method:boundary}).

Throughout, \methodname{} does not introduce a separate selection stage
operating on a materialized candidate pool; the same query-local graph and
the same fact-witness representation, together with the query-induced
obligations that the boundary enforces, drive both how evidence is activated
and how it is delivered to the reader.

\subsection{OpenIE Fact Substrate}
\label{sec:method:substrate}

The fact substrate $\mathcal{F}$ stores triples of the form
$f = (s, r, o)$ extracted from individual documents in $\mathcal{D}$, with
each triple anchored back to its source document via a provenance index.
$\mathcal{F}$ is constructed once during offline indexing and is reused
unchanged across all questions. Its role in \methodname{} is not to replace
documents as the unit of evidence: the reader still consumes natural-language
passages from $\mathcal{D}$. The substrate is the \emph{witness layer} for
document-to-document movement, supplying the auditable links that justify
why two documents may belong to the same evidence chain under a given
question.

Extraction follows the standard open information extraction setup used in
prior graph-based retrieval systems
\citep{angeli2015leveraging,gutierrez2025hipporag}: a language model
processes each document and emits its salient $(s, r, o)$ assertions, which
are then deduplicated and indexed alongside their source documents. We
deliberately keep the extractor fixed: the contribution of \methodname{} is
not a new fact extractor, but a different role for the facts it produces
--- as witnesses for document-level transitions rather than as the
reasoning substrate or as prompt augmentation (\S\ref{sec:method:transition}).
For the witness layer, this is the only corpus-level LLM extraction pass:
after indexing, no query triggers fresh OpenIE extraction, fact repair, or
chain generation over $\mathcal{D}$.

This separation of roles distinguishes \methodname{} from two adjacent
lines of work. \textbf{Knowledge-graph reasoning approaches}
\citep{sun2024tog,huang2026relink} treat the extracted relations as the
reasoning substrate itself --- either by traversing a pre-built KG under
LLM guidance \citep{sun2024tog} or by reconstructing missing facts at query
time to repair broken paths \citep{huang2026relink}; \methodname{} instead
keeps $\mathcal{F}$ fixed and uses it only as a witness layer, so that
retrieval does not depend on query-time fact generation or path repair.
\textbf{Query-time chain-mining approaches} \citep{peng2026neocorrag} keep
documents as the reader unit but invoke a language model on the retrieved
text to mine entity-relation chains as prompt augmentation; \methodname{}
avoids this query-time mining step, and chain structure instead arises
from the pre-extracted witnesses that gate document transitions in $G_q$
(\S\ref{sec:method:transition}).

\subsection{Question-Anchored Document Graph}
\label{sec:method:anchor}

Given a question $q$, \methodname{} instantiates the query-local document
graph $G_q = (V_q, A_q)$ by combining two complementary signals: a
\emph{question anchor} $A(q) \subseteq \mathcal{E}$ extracted from $q$,
where $\mathcal{E}$ is the entity vocabulary induced by the fact substrate
$\mathcal{F}$, and an \emph{entry signal} $\mathrm{Entry}(q) \subseteq
\mathcal{D}$ obtained from a standard dense or lexical retriever. The
anchor identifies which entities $q$ is asking about; the entry signal
identifies which documents are the most relevant starting points in
$\mathcal{D}$. Neither signal alone determines the evidence delivered to
the reader: $V_q$ is initialized from these two signals and then formed
where they can be connected through facts in $\mathcal{F}$.

Concretely, $V_q$ is initialized with the union of (i) documents in
$\mathrm{Entry}(q)$ and (ii) documents that are the provenance of at least
one fact in $\mathcal{F}$ whose arguments include an anchor entity in
$A(q)$. From this initial vertex set, $V_q$ is expanded through a bounded
number of hops along facts whose entities are connected to those already
activated, with the size of $V_q$ bounded to keep the activated region
compact relative to $\mathcal{D}$, while the reader budget $K$ ultimately
constrains $E_K$. This activation rule keeps $|V_q|$ small enough to make
downstream computation tractable, while still allowing documents that
are reachable only through multi-hop entity chains to enter $V_q$ even
when they have low surface similarity to $q$.

Two properties of this construction matter for the rest of the method.
First, the entry signal seeds activation but does not by itself determine
$E_K$. Documents in $\mathrm{Entry}(q)$ enter $V_q$ for consideration, but
their position in the entry ranking is not the sole criterion that decides
which of them reach the reader; what matters downstream is whether they
participate in fact-witnessed transitions
(\S\ref{sec:method:transition}). Second, the anchor extraction is the
mechanism by which $q$ exerts query-conditional control over $G_q$.
Specifically, anchors are extracted from entity mentions and
relation-bearing expressions in $q$, using the same controlled language
model used throughout our protocol. This extraction is applied to the
question, not to the retrieved corpus, and does not generate evidence
chains; it provides the question-side semantic handles that allow $G_q$
to grow toward documents that $q$ actually asks about, rather than only
toward documents that happen to score well on dense similarity.

\subsection{Fact-Witnessed Evidence Transitions}
\label{sec:method:transition}

We now turn to the core mechanism of \methodname{}: how the arcs $A_q$ of
the query-local graph are admitted. The aim is to make chain connectivity
a structural property of $G_q$, audited at the moment of admission, so
that the evidence chain reaching the reader is constructed within the
retrieval graph itself rather than reassembled from text afterward.

\paragraph{Witness sets.}
Let $f = (h, r, t) \in \mathcal{F}$ denote a fact extracted from some
source document $\mathrm{src}(f) \in \mathcal{D}$, with head and tail
entities $h, t \in \mathcal{E}$. For an ordered pair of documents
$(d_i, d_j)$, the \emph{witness set} under question $q$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:witness-set}
W_q(d_i, d_j) = \bigl\{\, w \in \mathcal{W}(\mathcal{F}) \;\bigm|\;
w \text{ links } d_i \text{ and } d_j \text{ under } q \,\bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{F})$ ranges over witness objects induced by
facts in $\mathcal{F}$: a single fact $f$ whose endpoints reach across
$d_i$ and $d_j$, or a pair of facts $(f_i, f_j)$ with
$\mathrm{src}(f_i) = d_i$ and $\mathrm{src}(f_j) = d_j$ that share an
activated argument. A witness $w$ links $d_i$ and $d_j$ under $q$ when one
of its arguments is activated by $q$ (either through the anchor set
$A(q)$ or through entities already entered the activated region of $V_q$)
and a complementary argument grounds the other document. An arc
$(d_i, d_j)$ is \emph{eligible} for $A_q$ when $W_q(d_i, d_j)$ is
non-empty; surface similarity between $d_i$ and $d_j$ or their individual
scores against $q$ may seed activation but are not by themselves
sufficient to create an arc.

\paragraph{Auditability.}
Every arc in $A_q$ therefore carries an explicit witness in $\mathcal{F}$:
each chain that ultimately reaches the reader can be traced back, edge by
edge, to the source facts and their provenance in $\mathcal{D}$. This is
what distinguishes \methodname{}'s notion of connectivity from a
similarity graph in which two documents are joined whenever they share
topic vectors: similarity may motivate co-occurrence, but it does not
establish that the two documents support a multi-hop step that $q$ asks
about. Witness sets provide this support by construction.

\paragraph{Witness modes.}
Within Eq.~\eqref{eq:witness-set}, two complementary modes of linkage
naturally arise. A \emph{sentence-grounded witness} is a single fact whose
head and tail are both anchored in a source sentence that already mentions
an entity activated by $q$; this captures cases where one document
explicitly bridges to the other through a $q$-relevant entity within a
bridging sentence. An \emph{argument-grounded witness} is induced by fact
arguments grounded in the two documents through a shared activated
entity, capturing cases where the multi-hop step is realized through
entity coreference across documents rather than a single bridging
sentence. Both modes share the same admission condition (a non-empty
$W_q$) and the same auditability property; they differ only in how the
witness is realized within $\mathcal{F}$.

\paragraph{Pre-extracted rather than generated witnesses.}
A crucial property of Eq.~\eqref{eq:witness-set} is that witnesses range
over $\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{F})$, which is induced by the pre-extracted
substrate from \S\ref{sec:method:substrate}, and not over text retrieved
at query time. At the point of arc admission, the query-conditional
information used to test witnesses is grounded in $A(q)$ and the current
state of $V_q$; witnesses themselves are not generated, paraphrased, or
re-extracted from $\mathcal{D}$ for $q$. Chain structure thus arises as a
property of the retrieval graph rather than as a relation reconstructed
from retrieved text at query time.

\subsection{Reader-Boundary Chain Integrity}
\label{sec:method:boundary}

The query-local graph $G_q$ constructed in
\S\ref{sec:method:anchor}--\S\ref{sec:method:transition} typically contains
more documents than the reader budget $K$ admits. At the reader boundary,
\methodname{} forms the evidence set $E_K$ of size $K$ as the
budget-bounded outcome of the same graph-formation process. Rather than
treating this as an independent ranking step performed over the activated
documents, we formulate it as a \emph{chain-integrity boundary} on $G_q$:
$E_K$ should preserve the witnessed connectivity already established in
$A_q$, and any document admitted into $E_K$ should either extend an
existing witnessed chain or close a question-induced obligation that no
document already in $E_K$ covers.

\paragraph{Question-induced obligations.}
For \methodname{} to determine whether a candidate document closes a part
of $q$ that the current $E_K$ does not yet cover, the chain-integrity
boundary is conditioned on a small set of \emph{question-induced
obligations}: entity mentions and relation-bearing slots that $q$ expects
the evidence to jointly resolve. These obligations are extracted from $q$
with the same controlled language model used for the question anchor in
\S\ref{sec:method:anchor}, applied to the question and not to the
retrieved corpus, and do not generate evidence chains. Their role is
purely local to the boundary decision: an obligation that is already
addressed by an existing document in $E_K$ does not require a further
admission, while an obligation still open after the current prefix
becomes a precondition for admitting an additional document.

\paragraph{Witness-preserving formation.}
Concretely, $E_K$ is formed incrementally from $G_q$ in two interleaved
modes. \emph{Witness-preserving admission} extends $E_K$ along arcs of
$A_q$, so that documents already supported by a witnessed transition to
some $d \in E_K$ are preferred to documents that would only enter $E_K$
through unwitnessed similarity. \emph{Residual admission} is reserved for
cases where an open obligation remains and a candidate document, not yet
connected to $E_K$ by a witnessed transition, provides evidence for it.
This second mode is what allows $E_K$ to cover the question's induced
relation structure even when the witnessed component of $G_q$ has not yet
reached every required document. Residual admission does not create new
unwitnessed arcs: once a document is admitted, any further movement
involving it in $G_q$ remains subject to the same witness conditions of
\S\ref{sec:method:transition}.

\paragraph{Boundary as continuation of graph formation.}
The chain-integrity boundary is not a separate scoring layer applied to a
materialized candidate set. The same graph $G_q$ and the same witness
layer $\mathcal{F}$ that drove activation in
\S\ref{sec:method:anchor}--\S\ref{sec:method:transition} also drive the
boundary; the boundary is additionally conditioned on the obligation set
extracted from $q$. $E_K$ is thus the reader-budget boundary of the same
graph-formation process that built $G_q$, rather than an independently
selected set produced by an external scoring procedure.
