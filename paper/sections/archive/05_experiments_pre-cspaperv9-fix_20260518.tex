\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We design experiments around three research questions that mirror the
framing of \S\ref{sec:intro}. \textbf{RQ1.} How does \methodname{}
compare with no-retrieval, sparse, dense, hierarchical, OpenIE-based,
and path/chain-oriented baselines on multi-hop QA under a unified reader?
\textbf{RQ2.} Which components of \methodname{}---the fact-source
substrate, query-local PPR, evidence-need mining, and
coverage-aware reranking---contribute to retrieval and answer quality,
and do they target the two failure modes (bridge underweighting and
entity-neighborhood crowding) identified in \S\ref{sec:intro}?
\textbf{RQ3.} Does the evidence flow change which passages enter the
top-five reader prefix, beyond reordering dense neighbors? We report
paired bootstrap confidence intervals, operational statistics, and
query-level case studies in the Appendix.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on three widely used multi-hop question answering
benchmarks: HotpotQA \citep{yang2018hotpotqa}, 2WikiMultiHopQA
\citep{ho2020constructing}, and MuSiQue \citep{trivedi2022musique}.
The main comparison also reports NQ and PopQA as open-domain QA checks.
Following the evaluation protocol of HippoRAG~2
\citep{gutierrez2025hipporag}, we use a 1{,}000-query split per dataset
together with the corresponding retrieval corpus. This setting allows
us to report retrieval quality and downstream answer quality under the
same reader protocol.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We group baselines into three categories that mirror the
\S\ref{sec:related} taxonomy.
\emph{(1) Classic RAG} ranks passages without an explicit
cross-document graph: a no-retrieval GPT-4o-mini control to expose
the reader's intrinsic capability, BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25}
as a sparse baseline, the dense entry ranking from NV-Embed-v2
\citep{lee2025nvembed}, RAPTOR \citep{sarthi2024raptor} as a
hierarchical-tree retriever, and IRCoT \citep{trivedi2023ircot} as
an iterative chain-of-thought retriever that performs up to three
retrieval steps under the same Qwen3-32B reasoning model. We
discuss iterative dense retrievers in \S\ref{sec:related}; they
require training and indexing choices that do not share our
controlled no-training setup, so Table~\ref{tab:main-results}
keeps the dense entry ranking as the pointwise retrieval control,
and we leave a head-to-head extension to multi-hop dense
retrievers such as \citet{xiong2021mdr} and \citet{zhao2021beamdr}
to future work.
\emph{(2) GraphRAG} systems propagate over a pre-built graph
representation. HippoRAG~2 \citep{gutierrez2025hipporag} runs PPR
over a passage--phrase graph, HGRAG \citep{hgrag2024} applies
hypergraph diffusion over entity and passage granularities, and
PropRAG \citep{wang2025proprag} performs beam search over a
proposition graph. We cite hierarchical multi-granular graphs
\citep{hu2026mgrangrag} and Relink-style query-time path
reconstruction \citep{huang2026relink} for completeness; these
were not run under our shared protocol and are not in
Table~\ref{tab:main-results}.
\emph{(3) Evidence-enhanced GraphRAG} refines the candidate set
that reaches the reader. NeocorRAG \citep{peng2026neocorrag} mines
evidence chains from retrieved text and uses them to filter the
reader context.
For methods that require language-model-assisted indexing, chain
extraction, summarization, or question-side analysis (RAPTOR,
IRCoT, HippoRAG~2, HGRAG, PropRAG, NeocorRAG, and \methodname{}),
we use Qwen3-32B with reasoning traces disabled in the output. For
final answer generation, all methods use the same GPT-4o-mini
reader.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Following \citet{gutierrez2025hipporag}, we evaluate two subtasks.
Retrieval is measured by supporting-passage recall in the top-five
reader context, denoted R@5. End-to-end QA is measured by exact match
(EM) and token F1 against the reference answer. For ablations, we
additionally report All@5, the fraction of questions for which all
gold supporting passages appear in the top-five reader prefix. All
scores are reported as percentages.

\paragraph{Implementation details.}
All retrieval systems produce ranked passage lists, and the reader
consumes the same top-five reader context across methods. All methods
are evaluated on the same 1{,}000 questions and the same corpus
snapshot for each benchmark. HippoRAG~2 and PropRAG retrieve from graph
candidate pools capped at 200 passages.
\methodname{} builds a top-100 native candidate pool from the same
top-200 frontier, performs query-local propagation
(\S\ref{sec:method:retrieval}) over its fact-source substrate
(\S\ref{sec:method:substrate}), and passes the reranked list
(\S\ref{sec:method:enhancement}) to the same reader. Dense embeddings
are computed with NV-Embed-v2 for the fact-source substrate, and
question-side anchors $A(q)$ and requirements $B(q)$ are extracted by
Qwen3-32B with a fixed prompt. The reader uses temperature 0, and
EM/F1 are computed under the same answer normalization across methods.
For RAPTOR, we build recursive summary trees with Qwen3-32B summaries
and NV-Embed-v2 embeddings, retrieve tree nodes, and project selected
summary nodes back to descendant leaf passages before applying the same
top-five reader protocol.
Hyperparameters $\eta$ and $\lambda$ are tuned once on a small
development split and held fixed across datasets. Prompts and the full
hyperparameter table are provided in the Appendix.
Appendix~\ref{app:baseline-protocol} details how baseline outputs are
adapted to the shared top-five passage-reader protocol.
We report these systems as controlled shared-reader adaptations rather
than paper-faithful reproductions of every original hosted-model setup.

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} presents the main comparison. We
analyse retrieval quality and answer quality separately, since the
two metrics decouple in several places.

\paragraph{Retrieval analysis (R@5).}
On retrieval, \methodname{} obtains the best R@5 on HotpotQA
(96.5) and 2WikiMultiHopQA (96.2), and is within $0.7$ R@5 of the
best path-based baseline on MuSiQue (73.5 vs 74.2 for PropRAG).
The largest absolute gain is on 2WikiMultiHopQA, where R@5 rises
from 75.9 (dense entry) and 71.7 (RAPTOR) to 96.2, and from 87.6
(IRCoT) and 90.9 (PropRAG) to 96.2. This is the dataset where
multi-hop bridges are most prevalent, and the gap supports the
\S\ref{sec:intro} claim that pointwise dense and hierarchical
text retrievers underweight bridges. Among GraphRAG baselines,
\methodname{} also improves R@5 over HippoRAG~2 ($+13.4$ on
2WikiMultiHopQA) and HGRAG ($+19.5$ on 2WikiMultiHopQA), isolating
the contribution of the fact-source propagation against
passage--phrase PPR and entity hypergraph diffusion. NeocorRAG, the
only Evidence-enhanced GraphRAG baseline in the table, saves
reader contexts of average size $3.3$--$3.6$ passages; under the
same top-five reader, \methodname{} improves average R@5 from
$85.7$ to $88.7$.

\paragraph{Answer-quality analysis (EM/F1).}
On QA, \methodname{} gives the best F1 on all three multi-hop
datasets and the best EM on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and
MuSiQue. The 2WikiMultiHopQA F1 of $74.7$ improves on PropRAG
($69.1$, $+5.6$) and on the dense entry ranking ($60.5$,
$+14.2$). The HotpotQA F1 gain over PropRAG is $+0.5$ and the
MuSiQue F1 gain is $+1.3$, smaller margins that remain positive
under the bootstrap intervals in
Appendix~\ref{app:bootstrap-ci}. We therefore characterise the
empirical contribution as a strong improvement on bridge-heavy
multi-hop retrieval, together with consistent but smaller deltas
on the remaining multi-hop settings, rather than a uniformly
large gain.

\paragraph{Open-domain checks.}
The NQ and PopQA columns in Table~\ref{tab:main-results} are
reported as auxiliary single-hop open-domain checks rather than as
the primary evaluation axis. On NQ, \methodname{}'s R@5 is lower
than several baselines while F1 is comparable; on PopQA, F1
improves over the dense entry baseline by a sizeable margin but
the picture across graph baselines is mixed. These results are
consistent with the design being targeted at multi-hop bridge
recovery, where the retrieval bottleneck is converting graph
relevance into a connected and covering reader prefix, and they
suggest that the same operators are not uniformly advantageous in
single-hop settings where pointwise dense retrieval is already
strong.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates the contribution of each
component inside \methodname{}. We tie each row to a claim from
\S\ref{sec:intro}. The \emph{w/o source edges} row is narrower than
deleting the entire substrate: it removes only the
provenance-grounded edges introduced in
\S\ref{sec:method:substrate} while leaving endpoint connectivity
and the downstream readout unchanged.

\paragraph{Local graph retrieval is the main retrieval engine.}
Removing local graph retrieval is equivalent to using the dense
entry ranking and produces the largest drop on 2WikiMultiHopQA,
where F1 decreases by $14.2$ points and All@5 by $40.2$ points.
This is consistent with the evidence flow $\pi_q$ being the core
retrieval signal, and with the two evidence-aware steps in
\S\ref{sec:method:enhancement} refining its ranking rather than
creating it.

\paragraph{Source edges help bridge recovery.}
Removing the source edges hurts 2WikiMultiHopQA most, reducing F1
from $74.7$ to $72.3$ and All@5 from $89.5$ to $80.3$. The smaller
changes on HotpotQA and MuSiQue suggest that endpoint connectivity
can sometimes preserve support recall on its own, but the sharper
2Wiki drop shows that provenance-grounded edges are important when
bridge passages must be admitted under a small reader budget.

\paragraph{Evidence-need mining targets bridge underweighting.}
Removing evidence-need mining hurts 2WikiMultiHopQA most clearly
(R@5 $-3.4$, F1 $-3.6$, All@5 $-8.4$), consistent with its role of
admitting bridge and tail-entity passages that lie far from the
query anchors and receive little $\pi_q$-mass. This is consistent
with the bridge-underweighting failure mode identified in
\S\ref{sec:intro}. The effect is smaller on HotpotQA, where bridges
are shorter, and mixed on MuSiQue: the F1 difference is within
$0.1$ point, while the full model still improves R@5 and All@5. We
therefore report MuSiQue as a retrieval-side gain rather than a QA
gain from evidence-need mining alone.

\paragraph{Coverage-aware reranking targets entity-neighborhood crowding.}
Removing coverage-aware reranking produces a more uniform retrieval
drop. R@5 decreases on all three datasets, and All@5 decreases by
$2.8$, $6.9$, and $3.1$ points on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and
MuSiQue respectively. This is consistent with the
entity-neighborhood crowding failure mode identified in
\S\ref{sec:intro}: local graph retrieval alone can leave the head
of the ranked list concentrated around a single entity
neighborhood, and the noisy-OR coverage objective improves the
final reader prefix by preferring candidates that contribute to
question-side requirements not yet represented.

\paragraph{Fact-source substrate isolation.}
The fact-witness ablation in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} removes only
the strongest provenance-grounded transitions while keeping the rest
of the substrate. To complement it, Table~\ref{tab:substrate-iso}
reports a stricter substrate-isolation experiment in which the entire
fact-source substrate (\S\ref{sec:method:substrate}) is replaced by a
phrase-source-only graph that retains source edges between phrases
and passages but drops the fact nodes; the same query-local PPR,
relation expansion, coverage reranking, and GPT-4o-mini reader are
applied unchanged. Replacing the substrate decreases multi-hop
average R@5 by $1.1$ points, multi-hop average All@5 by $2.7$
points, and average F1 by $1.1$ points. The largest coverage loss
falls on 2WikiMultiHopQA, where All@5 drops by $4.9$ points and F1
by $1.9$ points, while HotpotQA is essentially tied. We read this
as cautious evidence that promoting OpenIE facts to first-class
nodes contributes specifically to connected evidence coverage on
bridge-heavy questions, rather than to a uniform gain across all
multi-hop datasets.

\paragraph{Model adaptation across upstream extractors.}
A complementary check varies the upstream language model used for
OpenIE extraction, chain mining, and question-side analysis,
holding the GPT-4o-mini reader and NV-Embed-v2 encoder fixed.
Replacing Qwen3-32B with Qwen3-8B for all language-model-assisted
methods (Appendix~\ref{app:upstream-sensitivity},
Table~\ref{tab:upstream-sensitivity}) preserves the ranking of the
top systems: \methodname{} remains the strongest (multi-hop
average F1 $64.4$, vs. $63.4$ for PropRAG, $60.8$ for HippoRAG~2,
$60.5$ for HGRAG, $58.5$ for NeocorRAG). The largest 8B-to-32B gap
is for NeocorRAG ($+4.6$ F1 average), whose saved chain quality is
closely tied to the upstream model; \methodname{} drops $2.0$ F1
on average. This indicates that the gains in
Table~\ref{tab:main-results} are not an artefact of using the
larger Qwen3-32B extractor.

Table~\ref{tab:flow-diagnostic} addresses RQ3 by directly contrasting
the dense entry ranking with \methodname{} on the retrieval side.
The R@5, All@5, and support-gain columns are computed against gold
supporting-passage labels and are independent of the GPT-4o-mini
reader, so they isolate the retrieval-side contribution from any
reader effects discussed in Tables~\ref{tab:main-results} and
\ref{tab:ablation}.
\paragraph{Evidence flow changes the top-five evidence set.}
The largest gain appears on 2WikiMultiHopQA, where R@5 improves by
20.4 points and All@5 by 40.2 points. The support-gain column shows
that for 45.1\% of 2Wiki queries, \methodname{} retrieves strictly
more gold supporting passages in the top five than the dense entry
ranking. This is consistent with the core propagation step not simply
reordering the dense neighbors of the question, but changing
membership of the top-five list. The effect is smaller on HotpotQA,
where the dense baseline already retrieves most supporting passages,
and intermediate on MuSiQue.

\paragraph{Retrieval--QA coupling is not linear.}
Comparing Tables~\ref{tab:main-results} and \ref{tab:flow-diagnostic}
shows that retrieval and answer metrics are related but not identical.
On MuSiQue, PropRAG obtains the highest R@5 while \methodname{} obtains
the highest F1, indicating that better individual passage recall does
not always translate into better answer generation when questions
require longer or more fragmented evidence combinations. This is
consistent with the \S\ref{sec:intro} claim that a useful multi-hop
retriever should place a \emph{connected and covering} evidence set
into the reader prefix, not only recover individual gold passages.

\paragraph{Operational cost.}
Table~\ref{tab:main-efficiency} summarizes the main operational costs.
OpenIE extraction and graph construction are offline costs. At query
time, the induced graph remains small relative to the corpus: the
local graph averages about 200 passages and 1.4K--1.6K local edges, and
the evidence-aware readout adds at most 0.17 seconds per query
excluding the reader. Appendix~\ref{app:operational-stats} provides the
full offline/online breakdown, including Qwen3-32B binding latency.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and operational checks.}
Appendix~\ref{app:bootstrap-ci} reports query-paired bootstrap
intervals for the main deltas. The largest gains are stable under this
resampling. On 2WikiMultiHopQA, \methodname{} improves F1 over the dense
entry ranking by 14.2 points with a 95\% interval of [11.8, 16.6], and
improves R@5 by 20.4 points with an interval of [18.9, 21.9]. The
component-level intervals also separate the strong 2Wiki gains from the
smaller MuSiQue enhancement deltas, which we treat as retrieval-side
evidence rather than a robust QA gain. Appendix~\ref{app:operational-stats}
reports the corresponding graph and runtime statistics. Across the
three multi-hop datasets, the query-local graphs average about 200
passages and 1.4K--1.6K local edges, while the evidence-aware readout
adds 0.10--0.17 seconds per query excluding the reader. The offline
OpenIE substrate contains 68K--143K facts for the evaluated corpus
snapshots. Appendix~\ref{app:case-study} gives two query-level cases in
which the final readout admits a lower-ranked bridge passage into the
top-five prefix. Appendix~\ref{app:upstream-sensitivity} repeats all
language-model-assisted baselines and \methodname{} with Qwen3-8B in
place of Qwen3-32B; the ranking of the top systems is preserved and
\methodname{} remains the best system, indicating that the gains in
Table~\ref{tab:main-results} are not an artefact of using the larger
upstream extractor.
